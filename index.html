I am an MS student at the University of Utah, working with Professor Tucker Hermans in the LL4MA Lab. My research focuses on robotic manipulation under uncertainty: deciding what a robot must observe before it can act safely, and how learning can make those decisions more efficient.

Before Utah, I conducted machine-learning research with the Canadian Vertical Lift Autonomy Demonstration (CVLAD) team at the National Research Council Canada, and earned my Bachelor of Computer Science Honours, with High Distinction, at Carleton University.

Research & Projects

Information Gathering for Manipulation Under Occlusion

MS thesis research · University of Utah · July 2026–present

My thesis develops task-conditioned information gathering for retrieving a target object under occlusion. A fixed-camera robot first plans an optimistic retrieval trajectory, treating unknown space and other objects as free while avoiding the static environment. The space swept out by this motion defines the task-relevant region.

The system maintains a voxel occupancy belief and chooses between peeking—lifting an occluding object, observing behind it, and returning it—and relocating a blocking object to maximize expected information gain over that region. It executes the retrieval only once the region is fully observed and clear.

In kinematic simulation, the system retrieved the designated target in all 20 SceneReplica benchmark scenes, typically with at most one extra action. I also demonstrated the method on a real KUKA iiwa in two comparable tabletop scenes and am currently extending it to shelf scenes.

Real-Robot Pick-and-Place System

Robotics systems development · LL4MA Lab · September 2025–August 2026

After working with postdoctoral researcher Dr. Martin Matak on known-object perception, I became the sole developer of the pick-and-place system in January 2026. I extended his cuRobo-based planning wrapper into a full pipeline for a KUKA iiwa arm with a Robotiq 2F-140 gripper, adding trajectory preview with an abort option, joint pick-and-place planning, and grasp and placement re-scoring from the configurations the arm actually reaches.

I later integrated stereo depth, segmentation, and grasp generation for arbitrary objects, migrated the system from cuRobo v1 to v2, and redesigned hand-eye calibration using a custom 3D-printed ChArUco fixture and replayable calibration poses.

Helicopter Landing-Point Range Estimation

Honours thesis · Carleton University · Advisor: Professor Matthew Holden · 2025

A supervised machine-learning model that estimates the distance to a helicopter's final landing point from its flight trajectory and the landing site's lidar point cloud, using data from the NRC's Bell 412 research helicopter.

Helicopter Landing-Phase Identification

Machine-learning research · National Research Council Canada (CVLAD) · May–July 2024

As sole developer of the project's landing-detection models, I trained and compared supervised models on 6-DOF helicopter flight time-series data using MLP, CNN–MLP, RNN–MLP, and CNN–RNN–MLP architectures in TensorFlow/Keras, evaluating different input feature sets. I also developed a MATLAB app for data preprocessing and manual labeling, co-presented the results at CASI ASTRO 2024, and wrote the machine-learning sections of the lab's internal technical report.
